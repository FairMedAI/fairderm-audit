\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Background:} Dermatological deep learning models have been shown to exhibit performance disparities across skin tones, with diagnostic accuracy often degrading substantially for darker skin presentations \cite{Daneshjou2022}. Such biases threaten the equitable deployment of clinical decision support tools and may exacerbate existing health disparities in dermatology.

\textbf{Methods:} We evaluate melanoma classification bias on the Dermatology Data Initiative (DDI) dataset \cite{DDI2021}, which provides skin-tone annotations \cite{Fitzpatrick1988} enabling subgroup analysis. An EfficientNet-B0 model is pretrained on HAM10000 and fine-tuned on DDI. We then apply targeted augmentation using saved photometric and geometric transforms (color jitter, noise, elastic deformation, rotations; no generative model), with augmentation factors of 0$\times$, 2$\times$, 5$\times$, and 10$\times$, drawing images round-robin across all 29 source lesions at every factor. Stages 2 and 3 share learning rate, batch size, frozen layers, and epoch budget. Because augmentation changes the effective class ratio, we additionally re-run Stage 3 from a common checkpoint under three class-weighting regimes to separate the augmentation effect from the imbalance shift, and we repeat the full pipeline across 5 seeds. Generated images derive exclusively from training-split dark-skin melanomas ($n\,{=}\,29$, 10 variants each, 290 total), and their provenance is verified per image rather than assumed. Bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals (1{,}000 iterations) are reported throughout.

\textbf{Results:} The pretrained baseline achieved overall AUROC 0.662 and Dark subgroup AUROC 0.588. Fine-tuning produced overall AUROC 0.634 and Dark AUROC 0.547, while adding 290 train-only generated dark melanomas produced overall AUROC 0.639 and Dark AUROC 0.481. The paired Dark AUROC delta for augmentation was $-0.066$ (95\% bootstrap interval $[-0.151,0.008]$, $p\,{=}\,0.962$; one-tailed directional, $H_1$: Dark improvement $>0$, $p$\,$=$\,proportion of bootstrap $\Delta \leq 0$). Once ablation arms are matched on frozen layers and lesion coverage, generated-image quantity has no measurable effect on Dark AUROC (0.509, 0.513, 0.503, 0.497 at 0$\times$, 2$\times$, 5$\times$, 10$\times$) and monotonically reduces Light AUROC (0.784 to 0.697). Holding the fine-tuned checkpoint fixed and varying only the class-weighting regime gave Dark AUROC 0.484 (pre-augmentation \texttt{pos\_weight} 2.853), 0.478 (recomputed \texttt{pos\_weight} 0.742, same images), and 0.550 (balanced composition and \texttt{pos\_weight} 1.0 with 101 generated dark benign images added), narrowing the Light--Dark gap from $-0.222$ to $-0.141$. \textbf{Conclusions:} Photometric augmentation from a small set of source lesions did not close the skin-tone performance gap and reduced Dark AUROC relative to fine-tuning, under a class-weighting scheme that itself over-weights the augmented minority class; the arm study quantifies how much of that change is attributable to reweighting rather than augmentation. The findings are preliminary because the Dark test subgroup contains only 10 melanoma cases and there is no external validation. Larger cohorts, independent validation, and clinician review of generated-image realism are needed.
\end{abstract}
